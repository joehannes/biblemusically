Scripture, set to music — and finished.

Studio Lightkid turns a chapter of text into a song, a video, a short, an illustrated ebook and a printed product. It drives the AI services you already pay for, with your own keys, on your own machine.

Seven days free, no card

The trial is the whole application. Two things wait for a subscription: exporting your projects, and saving copies out of the app. Everything you make during the trial is kept safe inside it, and subscribing unlocks it where it already is — nothing to redo.

What it actually does

  • Writes lyrics from a chapter, in any language, in a style you pick or one it learns from you.
  • Generates the music — Suno through your own session, or ACE-Step and HeartMuLa on a free GPU.
  • Analyses the audio, generates an image per section, assembles the video, and uploads it.
  • Cuts a vertical short with a card pointing back at the full video — over a minute, because TikTok pays nothing below that.
  • Writes the publicity for each platform in that platform's own register, with its own cover art.
  • Joins a whole book of chapters into one long compilation with chapter markers.
  • Makes an illustrated, poetic edition as an EPUB that plays the song — the only ebook format that can.
  • Puts phrases and art on real products through Printify, and publishes them.
  • Runs the heavy work on somebody else's computer, so a phone can do all of it.

Guided, and it talks

Every screen has a guide that asks a few questions with sensible answers already chosen, and reveals only the part that matters now. It reads the questions aloud and takes spoken answers, in whichever of sixteen built-in languages you use — or one you type in yourself.

Honest about the parts that are awkward

  • The builds are unsigned. Windows and macOS will warn you. Check the SHA-256 on the release page — with an unsigned build that is the only thing that really tells you it is the right file.
  • iOS cannot be sideloaded like Android. Apple requires a signature; there is no "allow unknown sources". TestFlight or AltStore, both explained in the install notes.
  • Suno has no official API. The app uses your own signed-in session. It expires from time to time and needs re-capturing, and it can break when Suno changes their site — so there is an automatic fallback for exactly that.
  • Free AI tiers run out. OpenRouter allows fifty requests a day for a whole account. The app budgets against that rather than discovering it at midnight.

Your work and your data

Projects, media and API keys stay on your machine and in remotes you configure. What reaches the server is your account, your entitlement, anonymous usage counters, and anything you type into a bug report. Not your lyrics, not your songs, not your files. During the trial the anonymous measurement is part of the deal; once you are paying it is off unless you turn it on.
