decide, and a subscription unlocks it where it already is.

That restriction exists for one reason, stated plainly: without it the trial could be used forever by
making a new account and re-importing prepared work. It is not there to inconvenience you.

### 4.1 Usage measurement, stated honestly

**During the free week**, anonymous usage measurement is part of the deal: which views you opened, which
guides you used, and where you stopped. It is first-party — it goes to my own server and nowhere else.
It is how a one-person project finds out that step four of a guide loses everybody.

**Once you are paying, it is off unless you turn it on.** Being studied is a favour, and a favour is not
something to extract from a customer.

**Session recording** (Hotjar, a third party that can replay what happened on screen) is **always
optional, on both trial and paid**, and off until you switch it on. If you do switch it on, Hotjar's own
privacy policy applies as well as this one.

### 4.2 Crash reports are sent without asking

When the software throws an error, the error and its stack trace are sent automatically. They contain no
content of yours. A crash nobody reports is a crash nobody fixes.

If it is the first time a particular error has been seen, you may be asked what you were doing. That part
is a question, and you can ignore it.

You can turn automatic crash reports off in Settings. Please do not — but you can.

## Appendix: tax, plainly

Lemon Squeezy is the merchant of record. They collect and remit VAT and sales tax where it is due, and
your receipt comes from them. This means **you are not being asked to work out tax**, and I am not
collecting it from you directly.

*If you are the operator reading your own terms: this arrangement is deliberate, and it is what makes
selling to consumers in the EU workable for one person without registering for VAT in every member state.
Note that VAT on digital services follows the **customer's** country, not the seller's — so it would
otherwise be owed regardless of where you are resident. Income tax is a separate question and depends on
your residence, which is worth a one-off conversation with an Austrian tax adviser rather than an
assumption; Austria's habitual-abode test counts days across a calendar year, not per visit.*
